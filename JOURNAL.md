# September 1: The idea

I got an old vintage amplifier from my neighbor.It had to many problems,so i soldered out everything i could,and spoiler alert:I'm very much thankful i did.
I used it as my first case.Well, it looked goofy,not very much sealing, but was enough to hold together the DIY amplifier version #1.
I used some class d amplifiers along with a passive preamp.

# September 12: More power and own ps

I'm very thankful for this little project.I learned so many things that i have never done before.Having to work with AC is one of the experiences.
I was experiencing a lack of a power,and because the amperage was split apart,it was very noisy and barely survived on so little power.
Then the idea strike again:I had two laptop chargers laying around,and i was very lucky:both of them were around 20 something volts 4.6 amper or so,around 95w.
The first version looks a bit funny,but im very thankful that i did everything correct,and i have the chance to write this right now.
Because i dont have a 3d printer, i cant make a proper casing for the project so it took a lot of space.That was a very big problem.
It was also the first time i used a hot glue gun,i had to for the rca connectors, and the ac wire.

# October 3: The new look
I had enough with the size,so i was searching around my house for a casing .I found my old powersupply,that i save from my father's 30 years old pc.Before i used it for that,i tried to power it on.Big mistake!
That was the first time i set the breaker off.First time for everything i guess.After i got my head shouted off,i cleared the case ,and put everything inside.I also got to use some stuff i got from a electrical engineering store,such as a cool power divider wago,double switch,some plugs,wire and a very strong double sided tape.I also used the fuse from the vintage amplifier.I love diy projects,i can reuse so many things!! But some parts are safer to  get or make exatly for the proper use.(i need to replace the fuse,because the amparage is very close to the ps inrush current).

# October 26: Bass spikes and filtering

I wanted to get rid of the static noise and get some storage for the big bass drops.I made a bank out of two 4700mikroF 40v capacitors that i also got from the vintage amplifier.I searched them up,and apperently,they are very good quality.So i soldered them together with two filtering foil capacitors for the high pitch noise .Thats where problems really started to arrive.I didnt know anymore what caused what.The capacitors took way to much space,and my speakers were making weird noises so i had to disconnect it.Pretty much my first fail in this project.I spent a LOT of time searching for the problem.

# October 31: Proper modules and protection arrived

I ordered online from a Hungarian shop called Modulshop a speaker protection module,a dc noise filter,some cables,heat shrink and LEDs.
I put them together and i was having a lot of fun,because they worked from the first time i soldered them.I was finnaly making it more proper.I put heatshrinks everywhere i could,and i would like to put some on the ac part as well for safety in the future.The problem was that my ps was failing for drawing so much current,so i had to solder in a dedicated cheap led powersupply.The casing for it didnt fit ,so i hat to wrap it up in electrical tape.Not very proud of it.
